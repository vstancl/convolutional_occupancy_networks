# h5 step H5-3: ray-space evaluation

Evaluation of the ray decoders in ray space, with no training loop. Branch `h5`. Nothing in `src/`,
`phd_work/`, `csgbox` or `dexelize` was changed. The matching, the thin classes and the per-axis counts are
csgbox's own (`csgbox.ceilings.match`, `evaluate_axis`), so the numbers are comparable with the Gate B report.
The aggregation is a reimplementation, tested against `scripts/gate_ceilings.py`. No dependency was added.

Also in this step: test f of H5-2 now uses the excess (see step 0 below), and `H5RayModel` has `encode` and
`decode` methods so that a shape is encoded once and decoded in ray chunks.

## Step 0: entropy floor (H5-2 test f)

`dense_loss` returns `entropy_floor` (0.5 × mean binary entropy of `y` over the informative cells, plus
0.5 × the same over the other cells; occfrac only, 0 for binary and tsdf) and `excess = total − entropy_floor`.
The overfit criterion for occfrac_128 is now that the excess drops by more than 50 %. Result: 61.3 % (was
39.9 % on the total, which cannot exceed about 65 % because of the floor). hits_8 is unchanged (70 %).

## Interfaces

### `h5/eval_rays.py`

- `outputs_to_rayhits(target, outputs, ray_axis, thr=0.5, occ_snap=None) -> list[RayHits]` (x, y, z; rays
  kept in input order within each axis). Rules:
  - `hits_K` (also `hits_8_plane`): `t = BOX_LO + s_pred h`, `decode_slots(t, sigmoid(mask_logits), K, thr)`.
  - `occfrac_128`: `decode_occfrac(sigmoid(logits))`, with the optional snapping below.
  - `binary_128`: `decode_binary_array(sigmoid(logits) > thr)`.
  - `tsdf_128`: `decode_tsdf(2.5 h tanh(logits), z, h)`, with `z, h` from `csgbox.ceilings._sample_positions`,
    as `tsdf_values` uses them.
- `evaluate_shape(model, shape_id, split, rays="all" | (ray_axis, ray_index), thr, device, chunk_rays=8192,
  root, deltas, occ_snap) -> list[dict]`: encoder once, decoder in chunks. GT is `gt_hits(tree)` (float64),
  restricted to the same rays with `rays.subset_rays`, then `evaluate_axis(gt, dec, axis, (0.002, h/2), h)`
  per axis. The points given to the encoder are 10000 points of the cloud, chosen with
  `SeedSequence([0, shape_id])`, with noise 0.001 (the training setting).
- `evaluate_outputs(target, outputs, gt, ray_axis, ray_index, ...)`: the same, from outputs and GT. Used by
  `evaluate_shape` and by the tests.
- `aggregate(results) -> (summary, markdown)`: sums the per-axis `evaluate_axis` results and derives the
  precision, recall, F1 of all hits; the mean and p95 |dt| (gate formula); the hit-count error per ray; the
  thin recall per L/h bin for walls, gaps, walls and gaps together, and tangent chords (grazing, c < 0.5); and
  the population-weighted walls-and-gaps recall over the bins with L/h < 1 (pooled counts).
- `to_markdown(summary, deltas, title)`: the table.
- `val_metric(model, n_shapes=200, n_rays=4096, split="val", thr, device, occ_snap) -> summary`: the first
  `n_shapes` shapes of `val.lst` in list order, with `fixed_val_rays(i, n_rays)`. The metric is
  `summary["deltas"]["0.002"]["f1"]`.

## Choices (not obvious from the spec)

1. **Occupancy fractions and the literal rule.** `decode_occfrac` treats any fraction with `0 < f < 1` as a
   partial cell. The literal rule `sigmoid(logit(clip(y, 1e-6, 1 − 1e-6)))` gives `f = 1e-6` for an empty cell,
   which is partial. Each empty cell then becomes a thin wall, and the decode produces about 4 × 10⁶ hits per
   axis instead of about 1.5 × 10⁴. The literal rule is the default, as the spec asks. The option
   `occ_snap` sets fractions below it to 0 and above `1 − occ_snap` to 1 before decoding. Results on the 10 test
   shapes (below): `occ_snap = 1e-5` reproduces the gate exactly; `1e-4` differs on 5 of 491 520 rays; `1e-3`
   on 79. The constant is `OCC_SNAP_RECOMMENDED = 1e-5` in `eval_rays.py`. **This is a decision for you**: the
   default should probably become the snapped rule for the network, since a trained network will not output
   exact 0 or 1 either. I have not made that change.
2. **Mask probability threshold.** `decode_slots` keeps slots with probability strictly above `thr` (0.5).
3. **Points for the encoder** at evaluation are a seeded subset of the cloud with the training noise, so that
   the same shape gives the same numbers each time.
4. **Aggregation.** The "walls and gaps together" row sums the two classes per bin. The population-weighted
   row over `L/h < 1` is the pooled ratio of recalled to total counts, which is the population-weighted mean.

### Validation metric timing (test d)

Untrained models, GPU, 200 shapes of `val.lst`, 4096 fixed rays per shape. Wall time includes the GT
(`gt_hits` for three axes per shape) and the decoding.

| target | occ_snap | wall time | per shape | decoded hits (200 shapes) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hits_8 | – | 25.8 s | 129 ms | – |
| occfrac_128 | off (literal) | 67.0 s | 335 ms | 2.08 × 10⁸ |
| occfrac_128 | 1e-5 | 57.8 s | 289 ms | 2.08 × 10⁸ |

The untrained occfrac outputs sit near 0.5 for every cell, so every cell is partial, with or without
snapping, and the decode produces about 10⁶ hits per shape. The occfrac timing is therefore a property of
untrained output, not of the snapping. A trained network will decode to far fewer hits.

The untrained F1 at delta 0.002 is 0.0054 (hits_8) and 0.0073 (occfrac). These numbers are a check of the
pipeline only.

### Rays per shape: 512 vs 1024 (step 4)

Forward + backward + loss, batch 32 shapes × n_rays × 10000 points, fp32, RTX 4070. Median of 3 after one
warm-up. `h5/scripts/time_rays.py`.

| target | n_rays | time (s) | peak GPU memory (GiB) |
|---|---|---|---|
| hits_8 | 1024 | 0.317 | 6.30 |
| hits_8 | 512 | 0.209 | 4.28 |
| occfrac_128 | 1024 | 0.291 | 6.11 |
| occfrac_128 | 512 | 0.197 | 4.18 |

The 1024 rows agree with the H5-2 test g (0.316 / 0.300 s; 6.30 / 6.14 GiB). Halving the rays gives about
0.66 of the time and 0.68 of the peak memory, which is close to linear in the number of rays. The encoder time
was not measured separately.

## Notes and limits

- The matched counts in test a' use the float64 GT from `gt_hits`. The gate uses the same GT and the same
  decoders, so the agreement is by construction for the decoders; the test checks our assembly (rays, slots,
  the sigmoid and thresholds, the per-axis split).
- The mismatch on occfrac is in the literal decoding rule, not in the matching. The fix is the snapping
  option above, which needs a decision (choice 1).
- `evaluate_shape` calls `gt_hits` for all N² rays of each axis, then restricts them. For a few rays this is
  wasteful. It was not profiled, so the share of the `val_metric` time that goes to the GT is not known.
- `hits_8_plane` is evaluated with the same rules as `hits_8`, but no test a for it (no perfect outputs for the
  plane head), only the untrained run in test c.
