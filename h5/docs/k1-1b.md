# H5-K1-1b: binarize decoding for occfrac_128, h/2 tables, verdict

Evaluation only, no training. Shapes: the first 200 val shapes with all rays (the models) and the 200 gate shapes (the
ceiling).

## Design choice: the `binarize` decoding (`h5/occ_binarize.py`)

The literal `decode_occfrac` and the `occ_snap` settings give decoded/GT ratios of 3 to 25 on the trained model, so the
band selection could never choose an occfrac setting. That was unfair to occfrac. `binarize` is my design choice for a
decoding that keeps the hit count comparable:

- a cell is occupied iff `f >= 0.5`; the cells outside the box count as empty;
- at each change between neighbouring samples `a` (before) and `b` (after) along the ray, one hit:
  - outside -> inside (enter): `t = end_a + (1 - f_a - f_b) h`
  - inside -> outside (exit): `t = end_a + (f_a + f_b - 1) h`
- `t` is clipped to `[start_a, end_b]`. When the pair holds a single surface this conserves the material length, so a
  plane inside a cell gets its hit exactly (`h5/tests/test_occ_binarize.py`, 8 tests);
- enter or exit follows from the direction of the change.

The cost: a material or gap interval thinner than about h/2 never changes the binarized state, so it gives no hits.

`h5/eval_rays.py`: the setting `{"occ_mode": "binarize"}` (name `binarize`) is the last entry of the occfrac_128
decoding grid, after the `occ_snap` settings, so the existing band selection (`train._select`) can choose it.

## Ceiling (gate B, GT occfrac columns, M = 128, 200 gate shapes)

`h5/scripts/occ_ceiling_binarize.py` reuses the csgbox gate pipeline unchanged. Full tables per L/h bin at delta 0.002 and
h/2 are in `h5/results/k1_1/occfrac_ceiling.md`. Summary at 0.002:

| decoding | precision | recall | F1 | weighted recall L/h < 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `decode_occfrac` | 0.885 | 0.937 | 0.910 | 0.427 |
| `binarize` | 0.966 | 0.894 | 0.928 | 0.119 |

`binarize` is better overall (F1), but its ceiling for the thinnest features is low: the [0, 0.25) bins are at 0 and
[0.25, 0.5) is at 7e-5. Any comparison with hits_8 on thin features is capped by this ceiling.

## Re-evaluation of occfrac_128 (`~/PhD/runs/k1_1`)

`q0_report.py eval --grid` evaluates every setting of the grid and applies the band selection. The new files are
`eval/occfrac_128_it{40000,80000,120000}_grid.json`, and the old `eval/occfrac_128_it{N}.json` files are kept.
Command:

    .venv/bin/python h5/scripts/q0_report.py eval --root ~/PhD/runs/k1_1 --runs occfrac_128 \
        --its 40000,80000,120000 --split val --n-shapes 200 --grid

At every checkpoint `binarize` is the only setting in band, and it is the one selected:

| iteration | binarize F1 | decoded/GT | best occ_snap F1 (decoded/GT) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40000 | 0.154 | 0.884 | 0.100 (3.91) |
| 80000 | 0.195 | 0.900 | 0.121 (3.25) |
| 120000 | 0.210 | 0.908 | 0.128 (3.10) |

## Report and verdict (`h5/results/k1_1/report.md`)

`k1_1_report.py` adds a row `occfrac_128 (grid)`, the per-run thin-bin tables at h/2 (labelled "descriptive, not the
criterion") and the verdict. The verdict compares hits_8 with the best F1 among the dense runs in band, at iteration
120000 and delta 0.002, using the SPEC rule (csgbox/SPEC.md section 5). Thresholds: +0.05 weighted recall of the L/h < 1
bins, F1 not lower by more than 0.01, and at most +0.02 in every bin to refute.

Result: occfrac_128 (grid) and points_strong are both in band, and points_strong has the higher F1 (0.232 against 0.210).
**Undecided (the SPEC calls this "compactness only")**: hits_8 gains +0.069 in weighted thin recall for L/h < 1 (0.129
against 0.061), which meets the 0.05 threshold. But its F1 is 0.056 lower (0.176 against 0.232), outside the 0.01
tolerance, so H5 is not supported. It is not refuted either, because the largest per-bin gain is +0.084.

Deviations from the SPEC, as stated in the report: TSDF M = 128 was not trained, so the best dense run in band stands
in for it. The t/h > 2 condition is checked with the F1 of all hits. IoU and the "smaller output" condition are not
evaluated.

Report fix: the per-checkpoint column that was labelled "weighted recall L/h < 1" is the M of H5-6 (walls and gaps
only), not the all-thin-features recall the verdict uses. It is now labelled `M (H5-6, walls+gaps L/h < 1)`.
